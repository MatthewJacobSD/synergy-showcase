01 / Context

What this artifact is about

This artifact explores how three students use AI in their studies — and where they draw the line. Each of us wrote a personal AI usage plan (Part 1), then demonstrated it through a real prompt scenario (Part 2).

The common thread: AI is a tool, not a shortcut. We each use it differently — Matthew for code and writing, Elina for language support, Thijmen for research and programming — but the principle is the same. We think first, check what AI gives us, and stay responsible for the final result.

02 / Core Concept

What is GiGo?

Garbage In,
Garbage Out.

AI does not magically clean bad input. It repeats and dresses up whatever you feed it.

If the question, the prompt, or the information you give to an AI is messy, vague, wrong, or incomplete, the answer you get back will also be messy. You will get "garbage" that looks professional but is not reliable.

The fix is not to avoid AI. It is to give it better input — and check the output before you use it.

05 / Reflection

What we learned

AI is not bad. The person who uses it may make it feel bad. If you put garbage in, you get garbage out. If you put a real question in, check the answer, and learn from it, then it can be growth out.

Each of us found a different balance between using AI effectively and staying independent. The key is not to avoid AI — it is to use it intentionally, check the output, and stay the person who decides what is correct.
