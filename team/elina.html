Part 1 — My AI Usage Plan

How I use AI

Since English is my second language, I sometimes find the meaning of certain words, sentences, or concepts unclear. I would like to use AI to help me better understand English materials throughout my education.

Intended use during studies

  • Ask AI to explain lessons, concepts, or English texts in my first language when I find them confusing
  • Use AI to get feedback on my work and understand what I can improve
  • Before giving a presentation, ask AI to check my work and give suggestions
  • Ask AI to give me ideas when writing a text and I need help developing ideas
  • Use AI to help me plan my studies and organize my study time

What responsible use looks like

Responsible use of AI means making sure that I do not become dependent on it. At every stage of my education, I want to think about the problem myself first. If I face difficulties, I can then use AI as a guide or a source of support. I do not want AI to do the work for me; instead, I want to use it as a helpful tool or an online teacher.

How I will avoid GiGo

  • Explain clearly what I want from AI and make my request as specific as possible
  • Use correct writing and clear sentence structures
  • Try to explain my question in simple language instead of complicated sentences
  • Do not completely depend on the first answer — ask follow-up questions
  • Communicate in my first language when it helps express thoughts more clearly
Part 2 — Prompt Scenario

Java translation pipeline

The project

A Java application that translates English sentences to Dutch through four modules: model (data structures), analysis (syntactic tagging), translation (dictionary lookup), and rules (grammatical restructuring).

How a sentence travels through the system

  1. The entry point receives an English text string from the user
  2. Sentence Analyzer parses input into Token instances with part-of-speech labels
  3. The application displays the analyzed structure to the terminal
  4. Translator references the dictionary CSV to map tokens to Dutch
  5. The direct translation result is outputted for display
  6. The user picks a grammar rule option from the console
  7. RuleEngine processes tokens according to the selected rule
  8. The final Dutch sentence structure is rendered

AI Interaction 1

What I needed: Clarify how Sentence Analyzer handles words missing from the CSV lookup files.

What AI told me: Unlisted words are given a default role (like 'Other') so the pipeline doesn't break.

What I did with it: Noted this behaviour to understand why unknown words can still pass through without exceptions.

AI Interaction 2

What I needed: Understand the design reason behind separating translation logic from syntax manipulation.

What AI told me: Keeping them separate follows the Single Responsibility Principle, making it easier to add new grammar rules.

What I did with it: Used this insight to write the architecture summary and better grasp how modular design is applied.
