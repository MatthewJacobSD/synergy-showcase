Part 1 — My AI Usage Plan

How I use AI

I honestly use AI for most of my personal work, even for basic things. The first time I discovered artificial intelligence was during an apprenticeship in the UK, and I was shocked by how convenient the usage was. From that day on I started to use it for simple tasks like refactoring code or commenting my code — basically the stuff I do not desire to do all the time.

One day I started to use it slowly, to a certain degree that I couldn't say no to it due to the convenience. Even now I am quite satisfied with its usage, although it did bring me into uncomfortable situations where I became aware of my heavy usage. When I tried to do what I used to do without it, I simply couldn't do it. But I gained a new view — I became more aware and analytical. What I used to struggle to understand, I understood quickly with its help.

Intended use during studies

  • To explain concepts I do not understand yet
  • To refactor, comment or check code
  • To restructure texts and improve grammar
  • To generate ideas or outlines when I am stuck
  • Not as a replacement for thinking the whole assignment myself

What responsible use looks like

Responsible use, for me, does not mean pretending I never use AI. It means I stay the person who decides what is correct. I use AI as a helper, not as the author of my thinking. I check what it gives me against what I already know. I try to understand the output — if I cannot explain it in my own words, then I did not learn it.

What GiGo is

GiGo means Garbage In, Garbage Out. If the question, the prompt or the information I give to an AI is messy, vague, wrong or incomplete, the answer I get back will also be messy. AI does not magically clean bad input. It repeats and dresses up whatever I feed it.

How I will avoid GiGo

  • Write clearer prompts, with context, instead of dumping a vague question
  • Not accept the first answer as final
  • Check facts, code and sources myself
  • Keep the useful parts and throw away the rest
  • Rewrite in my own language so I actually understand it
Part 2 — Prompt Scenario

Building a website

Bad input Fails

Build me a website about a tech store that sells tech media and generate a generous amount of revenue. Ensure the website is modern and follows web principles, integrating specific language suitable for sections related to the current project idea.

Why it fails

The main problem is that it does not give enough specific information about what I want. Words such as "modern", "web principles" and "suitable language" can mean different things depending on the project. The AI therefore has to make its own assumptions, which can lead to a result that does not match the original idea.

Better input Plan first

Build a website for a technology store that sells technology products and related media. This is for a university project, so first understand the concept before implementing anything. The target audience should be students and general consumers interested in technology.

First identify the purpose, target audience, website structure, required pages, content, visual style and main user journey. Do not immediately start implementing. First create a plan and explain any assumptions or missing information. After implementation, audit the website against the original requirements. Leave the final decision to the human reviewer.

Why it works better

This is better because I am giving the AI more information about the what, how, details, concept, goal, what to avoid and what to include instead of expecting it to understand everything from a short sentence. It also gives it a process to follow.

Verification checklist

  • Does it follow the original idea? Compare with what was originally wanted.
  • Does it follow the requirements? Check required pages, sections and functionality.
  • Is the content correct? Check whether AI invented information.
  • Does the design make sense? Check layout, navigation, typography and consistency.
  • Does it work on different screens? Test desktop, tablet and mobile.
  • Does the functionality work? Manually test buttons, navigation, forms.
  • Check the code. Inspect for errors, unnecessary code, duplicated code.
  • Human verification. Personally review the result.
  • Give the AI feedback and test again. If wrong, explain and refine.
  • Human decision. Decide if it is good enough to continue.
